The idea

The game on screen
and in the room

A game music video (GMV) that runs through three Devil May Cry games, with live-action shots that copy what the characters do on screen.

It opens on the last scene of Devil May Cry 3, where Dante walks into the shop he is about to name. As the scene plays, the cut jumps to the same actions filmed in real life: a door opening, a sword picked up, boots going up on a desk. When the neon sign lights up, the music takes over.

From there it runs through Devil May Cry 5 with Nero, drops into Devil May Cry 4 for the quiet part, and ends on Vergil walking back in.

Editing decisions

Cutting to the action
Cutting to the song

Music

Three themes, one track

A lullaby mix of Devils Never Cry sits under the opening for just over a minute. Devil Trigger starts quietly beneath the last lines of the scene and reaches full volume as the picture cuts to Devil May Cry 5. Shall Never Surrender comes in under Devil Trigger’s last bars and carries the ending.

Length

Shorten the song, keep the beat

Devil Trigger comes in 50 seconds into the track. I cut another 26 seconds from the middle and covered the join with a one-second crossfade. Everywhere else the song runs unbroken, so the picture could be cut against it freely.

Lyrics

Words that land on time

The song comes from its lyric video, with picture and sound kept linked on the bottom track. Trailer and gameplay footage sit on the tracks above. Wherever I left a gap, the lyric video shows through, so the words on screen are the words being sung.

Sound

Keep the games audible

The narration from the Devil May Cry 3 ending plays over the lullaby. The Devil May Cry 5 trailer keeps its own sound on a second track under the song, and twice it takes over for a second or two before the song returns, still in time. The live-action sword shot plays its audio from five stacked tracks, about 14 dB louder than one.

Plan and result

What changed
along the way

The outline the three of us wrote was a starting point. These are the parts that moved.

Length
Planned at about two minutes. Finished at 3:50.
Music
Planned around one song, Devil Trigger. Finished with three themes, from Devil May Cry 3, 4 and 5.
Opening
Planned as a low close-up of legs, then a sword being drawn. Finished on the ending of Devil May Cry 3, matched with live action.
Ending
Planned as a final gameplay strike. Finished on the low walking shot dissolving into Vergil’s walk.
Edit
Planned with a teammate leading. I ended up cutting the whole video.

Inside the edit

The Resolve project

Five video tracks and eight audio tracks. The finished cut uses 85 clips and 23 dissolves and crossfades. The live-action shots sit on their own track above the game footage, each one corrected on the Color page, and one adjustment clip carries a single look across the last 15 seconds.
